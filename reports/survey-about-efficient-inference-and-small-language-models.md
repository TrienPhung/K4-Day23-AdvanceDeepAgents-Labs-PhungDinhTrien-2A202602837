# Efficient Inference and Small Language Models: A Survey

## TL;DR
- “Small” is a deployment-relative label rather than a stable parameter threshold: one survey operationalizes it as 100M–5B decoder-only Transformers while emphasizing that device capacity shifts the boundary.[1]
- SLM capability depends on more than shrinking a large model: architectural choices, carefully curated or expanded training data, distillation, and tuning are complementary levers.[2][3][4][5]
- Inference efficiency spans model compression, fewer sequential generation steps, and better serving-memory utilization; these target different bottlenecks and are not interchangeable.[6][7][8][9]
- On-device applications make latency, memory, energy, privacy, and task-specific quality jointly important; general task scores alone do not establish deployment fitness.[10][11][12]

## Background
Small language models (SLMs) are models designed to perform language tasks with constrained computational resources, often for mobile, on-device, and edge use.[10] There is no universal size cutoff: a recent measurement survey chooses 100M–5B parameters as its scope for decoder-only Transformers, but explicitly notes that “small” changes as available device memory grows.[1] This makes SLM research a joint question about model capability and the hardware, runtime, and workload in which that capability is delivered.

The core motivation is to move useful generation closer to constrained settings, where remote inference may face latency, unreliable connectivity, privacy, bandwidth, or cost concerns.[10][13] Accordingly, efficiency should not be collapsed to parameter count. Evaluation surveys distinguish latency and throughput, memory footprint, and energy-related measures; they also discuss privacy as part of the deployment context.[10][14] A model can be compact yet unsuitable for a target device or task if its runtime costs, context needs, or task performance do not fit the use case.

The technical lineage includes compression and teacher–student learning. Deep Compression combines pruning, trained quantization, and Huffman coding, reporting large storage reductions in its evaluated setting.[6] Knowledge distillation transfers information from an ensemble into a single, less cumbersome network.[15] These are distinct ideas: compression changes representation or stored parameters, while distillation uses a teacher signal during learning. Contemporary SLM practice builds on both while making data and deployment constraints explicit.[2][5]

## Model construction: architecture, data, and supervision
A first route to an efficient model is to design for the target scale rather than merely shrink a larger architecture. MobileLLM focuses on sub-billion-parameter mobile models and highlights deep, thin architectures, embedding sharing, and grouped-query attention as design choices for that regime.[4] These choices matter because the desired outcome is not only fewer parameters but a useful model under mobile resource limits.

A second route is to invest in training data. SmolLM2 is a 1.7B-parameter model whose report attributes strong performance to overtraining on diverse datasets and compares it favorably with other recent small models.[2] Phi-3-mini, at 3.8B parameters, attributes competitive performance with larger models to an enhanced training dataset and alignment.[3] These reports suggest that training recipe and data quality are central dimensions of SLM capability, not post-hoc details. The results should not be read as a controlled proof that a single recipe transfers across all sizes or tasks: the available summaries describe model-specific reports rather than a shared causal benchmark.

Teacher-based methods provide another means of shaping a limited-capacity learner. MiniPLM refines the training-data distribution using offline teacher inference as a way to improve pretraining efficiency and effectiveness.[5] This approach places teacher computation upstream, rather than assuming the deployed student must carry a teacher at inference time. Instruction tuning and preference optimization are also recipe-sensitive: SmolTulu reports strong instruction-following and mathematical-reasoning results among sub-2B models in association with optimizing learning-rate-to-batch-size ratios.[16] Across these examples, architecture, data, and optimization are complementary axes; there is no single “small model” trick that replaces evaluation against intended tasks.

## Inference efficiency: compression, decoding, and memory
Once model weights are trained, compression can reduce storage and/or inference cost. The older Deep Compression pipeline combines pruning, quantization, and entropy coding, whereas GPTQ is a one-shot post-training weight quantization method using approximate second-order information.[6][7] GPTQ reports quantizing a 175-billion-parameter model to 3 or 4 bits with negligible accuracy degradation in its evaluated setting and reports inference speedups over FP16 on two GPUs.[7] The result illustrates the potential of low-bit weights, but those reported outcomes concern particular hardware and a much larger model; they should not automatically be treated as SLM device benchmarks.

Speculative decoding addresses a different constraint: autoregressive generation’s sequential target-model steps. It lets a faster model propose several tokens for parallel verification by the target, and the original method states that output sampling remains equivalent to the target model.[8] The method trades the cost and quality of drafting against fewer serial target steps; it is therefore most relevant when the additional draft work is outweighed by reduced target-model decoding time. For an already-small model, the value of adding a draft model is workload- and system-dependent rather than guaranteed by the method alone.

Serving systems also need to manage the key–value (KV) cache, whose per-request allocation can fragment memory or duplicate data and constrain batch size.[9] PagedAttention applies a paging-inspired memory-management design and underlies the vLLM serving system.[9] This complements quantization and speculative decoding: weight quantization targets model representation, speculation targets serial generation, and paged cache management targets memory use and concurrency. An optimization in one layer does not necessarily resolve bottlenecks in another.

## Deployment and evaluation: task fit is part of efficiency
SLMs are attractive for applications where local operation and constrained resources matter. TinyLLM studies task-specific edge sensing with models of 30M–124M parameters, and reports comparisons against billion-parameter models on IoT sensor datasets, including comparable accuracy with fewer GPU resources and less training time in its setting.[13] SlimLM explores document summarization, question answering, and question suggestion on a Samsung Galaxy S24, examining model size, context length, and inference time.[11] Such examples emphasize domain fit: a small model can be valuable for a constrained, scoped application without matching a general-purpose large model on every capability.

Evaluation therefore needs both task and systems measures. A measurement survey evaluates capabilities such as commonsense reasoning, mathematics, in-context learning, and long-context tasks, alongside on-device runtime measures including latency and memory.[1] Another SLM survey catalogues inference time and throughput, peak memory, and energy-efficiency measures.[10] SmartBench provides a complementary, application-specific example: it evaluates Chinese smartphone-assistant tasks across five categories and 20 tasks, and assesses quantized models deployed on smartphone NPUs.[12] This kind of task-specific evaluation can reveal requirements that aggregate scores may hide.

The right efficiency claim is consequently conditional: which workload, device, runtime, context length, and quality threshold are being measured? For instance, SlimLM’s reported experiments explicitly examine context length and inference time, while SmartBench targets mobile tasks and actual phone hardware.[11][12] An SLM survey identifies the balance between efficiency and performance as an unresolved concern.[14] This tradeoff can constrain deployment even when a model fits in memory.

## Trends and open problems
Recent SLM work, as represented in these sources, puts more emphasis on data and training recipes as well as architecture. SmolLM2 and Phi-3 link compact-model performance to diverse or enhanced training data and alignment, while MobileLLM foregrounds architecture at sub-billion scale.[2][3][4] Distillation-based data refinement and recipe tuning add further levers.[5][16] This is a shift from treating “small” as only a compression target toward designing the full training pipeline for a capacity and use case, although the cited summaries do not establish a common benchmark for comparing these recipes.

On the inference side, the sources show distinct optimization targets rather than one universal speedup: low-bit quantization, speculative decoding, and KV-cache management address weights, sequential token generation, and serving memory respectively.[7][8][9] Important open problems include fair comparisons across hardware and workloads, the quality–latency–memory–energy tradeoff, and whether task-specific gains generalize beyond their application domain.[10][12][14] A useful future evaluation should therefore report both quality and end-to-end system costs on representative devices and tasks, without assuming that parameter count alone predicts practical efficiency.[1][10]
